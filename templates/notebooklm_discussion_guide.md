## CUSTOM PROMPT FOR NOTEBOOKLM AUDIO OVERVIEW

Copy this into the "Customize" box when generating your podcast:

```
Create a podcast where the two hosts debate the key tensions in this macro data.
Focus on:

1. THE PARADOX: Economic data looks fine (unemployment 4.1%, inflation at 2.1% 
   on 3-month basis, Fed already cut to 3.63%) BUT consumer sentiment is crashing 
   at 49.8. Debate: Is the data wrong or are the consumers wrong?

2. THE FED'S DILEMMA: Inflation is basically at target but consumers are miserable. 
   Should the Fed cut more aggressively to save sentiment, or hold because the 
   numbers say they're done?

3. GOLD vs REAL YIELDS: Gold at $4,173/oz is down 10% this month despite war and 
   crisis. Real yields at 2.07% are the dominant force. Debate: Is gold broken as 
   a crisis hedge, or is this a buying opportunity?

4. HORMUZ FATIGUE: Iran declared closure AGAIN today (4th time). Oil barely moved. 
   The market is desensitized. Debate: Is this complacency or correct pricing?

5. SILVER OPPORTUNITY: Silver down 17% this month, near 200MA support, 4% crash 
   probability. Is this a buy or a falling knife?

Make the hosts disagree on at least 2 of these 5 topics. Use specific numbers from 
the data. Reference real events (Iran war, Fed rate path, consumer sentiment crash).
End with what each host is watching next week.
```

## DISCUSSION QUESTIONS (for NotebookLM Q&A mode)

After generating the podcast, paste these into NotebookLM's chat to go deeper:

1. "What would have to be true for consumer sentiment to recover from 49.8?"
2. "If the Fed cuts to 2.5% by year-end, what happens to gold, Bitcoin, and equities?"
3. "Compare the current macro setup to 2007 (before the GFC). What's similar? What's different?"
4. "If the Strait of Hormuz actually stays closed for 30+ days, what's the oil price target?"
5. "The gold/silver ratio is 64.3. What's the historical average and what does the current level signal?"
6. "Unemployment is 4.1% but consumer sentiment is 49.8. Has this divergence ever happened before? What happened next?"
7. "If you had $100,000 to deploy this week across gold, silver, Bitcoin, oil, and equities, how would you split it based on this data?"

## SOURCE COMBINATION STRATEGY

For the BEST podcasts, upload 3-5 sources to the same NotebookLM notebook:

### Tier 1: Your Core Analysis (always include)
- `reports/weekly/macro_briefing_YYYY-MM-DD.md` (our weekly briefing)

### Tier 2: Real-World Context (add 1-2)
- A Reuters or Bloomberg article from this week
- A Fed speech or FOMC statement (if released this week)
- URL to a relevant Wikipedia article (e.g., "2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis")

### Tier 3: Contrarian View (add 1)
- An article or blog post that DISAGREES with our analysis
- A YouTube transcript from a different perspective
- A research report with a different conclusion

### Why this matters:
NotebookLM creates dialogue between two AI hosts. If all sources agree, the podcast 
is boring. If sources disagree, the hosts debate, and the podcast is engaging.

The CONTRARIAN source is the secret weapon. Even if you disagree with it, it forces 
the AI to explore both sides of the argument.
