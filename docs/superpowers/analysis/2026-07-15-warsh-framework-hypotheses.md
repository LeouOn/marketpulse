## 1. The Framework

Kevin Warsh is executing what he calls a "regime change" in monetary policy. His framework, as articulated in his July 14-15 congressional testimony:

### Core elements:
- **Defeat inflation**: Pledged to make the "inflation tax" of the last five years "a thing of the past"
- **2% target**: Explicitly defended the 2% inflation target, criticized past Fed Chairs for not doing enough
- **Balance sheet restructuring**: Assembled a panel of senior academics and former policymakers to review the $6.7T balance sheet
- **"QE without QE"**: Use reserves management purchases (T-bills) to maintain liquidity while shrinking long-end holdings
- **Not mission accomplished**: "Latest improvement on inflation isn't mission accomplished"

### The transmission mechanism:
The framework depends on **yield curve steepening** to work:
1. Fed buys T-bills (short end) → contains 2Y yields
2. QT shrinks long-end holdings → 10Y/30Y bear risk premium
3. Result: 2Y stable, 10Y rises → spread widens
4. Banks borrow short at contained rates, buy long at higher yields → carry profitable
5. Banks absorb Treasury supply → "QE without QE" transmission works
6. Credit expands → economy grows → value rotation begins

**Critical dependency**: If the curve doesn't steepen (stays flat at +30-50bps), the transmission fails. Banks have no carry incentive. The handoff from Fed to private sector doesn't happen.

## 2. The Political Context

### Trump-Powell-Warsh dynamic:
- Trump demanded rate cuts throughout Powell's tenure
- Trump instructed DOJ to conduct a criminal investigation on Powell for not cutting rates
- Powell was demoted from Chair to Governor (still on FOMC, still voting)
- Trump appointed Warsh to replace Powell
- The day after Warsh's appointment, markets rallied (calling the bluff — betting Warsh would be dovish)
- Warsh has since **doubled down on hawkish rhetoric**, defending the 2% target

### Key tension:
Trump demands rate cuts → appointed Warsh → Warsh is hawkish. This is either:
- A miscalculation by Trump (Warsh is genuinely hawkish)
- A pantomime (Warsh talks hawkish, acts dovish)
- A transition (Warsh starts hawkish, becomes dovish over time)

## 3. Three Hypotheses

### Hypothesis A: Warsh is a genuine hawk (35% probability)

**Description**: Warsh truly believes in fighting inflation and the 2% target. His rhetoric is not theater — he means it. The balance sheet shrinks aggressively. QT continues. Rates stay higher for longer.

**Evidence FOR:**
- Doubled down on hawkish rhetoric in testimony (defended 2%, criticized past Chairs)
- Academic background at Hoover Institution — genuine monetary conservative
- Markets initially called the bluff → Warsh proved them wrong with MORE hawkishness
- Coming "out of the gates" hawkish suggests conviction, not performance

**Evidence AGAINST:**
- Trump demands rate cuts — appointed Warsh to deliver them
- DOJ investigation of Powell shows Trump punishes non-compliant Fed Chairs
- Sustained hawkishness conflicts with $35T+ national debt servicing costs
- AI capex cycle needs accommodative policy to continue

**Curve implication**: 2s10s stays flat at +30-50bps for 12-18 months. Value style continues to struggle.

**Confirming signals:**
- Balance sheet panel recommends aggressive QT with explicit size targets ($5T or lower)
- Warsh raises rates or holds firm despite political pressure
- Reserves management purchases remain minimal/temporary
- Powell (as governor) votes against easing

**Disconfirming signals:**
- Warsh pivots to "ample reserves" language without specific targets
- QT slows or pauses before balance sheet reaches stated targets
- Any rate cut within 6 months despite hawkish rhetoric

---

### Hypothesis B: Warsh is a pantomime (20% probability)

**Description**: Warsh was appointed specifically because he can SOUND hawkish while DOING dovish. The hawkish rhetoric provides political cover. Behind the scenes, shadow easing through reserves management purchases, expanded repo facility, and slowed QT.

**Evidence FOR:**
- Trump's entire history shows he demands rate cuts
- DOJ investigation proves Trump punishes non-compliant Chairs
- "QE without QE" framework is literally designed to ease without calling it easing
- Market initially called the bluff (rallied day after appointment)

**Evidence AGAINST:**
- Warsh is pushing harder than a pure pantomime actor would
- Defending the 2% target SPECIFICALLY is a credibility commitment — hard to walk back
- Criticizing past Chairs is an unusual move for someone planning to be MORE dovish
- The hawkish rhetoric creates expectations that shadow easing would violate

**Curve implication**: 2s10s steepens to +60-80bps within 3-6 months as market figures out the pantomime. Value rotation comes sooner than consensus expects.

**Confirming signals:**
- Balance sheet panel recommends "ample reserves" framework without specific targets
- Reserves management purchases expand and become permanent
- QT slows dramatically or pauses
- Standing repo facility expanded significantly
- Warsh's language gradually shifts from "fighting inflation" to "sustaining expansion"

**Disconfirming signals:**
- Warsh raises rates or tightens policy materially
- Balance sheet shrinks toward $5T
- Inflation drops below 2.5% AND Warsh still holds rates

### Hypothesis C: Warsh transitions from hawkish to dovish (45% probability)

**Description**: Warsh starts genuinely hawkish — establishing credibility and independence (responding to "Trump puppet" criticism). Over 6-12 months, fiscal reality (debt servicing), political pressure (Trump), and economic reality (AI capex needs) force a pragmatic pivot. The 2% target becomes fiction — inflation drifts to 3-3.5% through tolerance. The "regime change" language provides cover for redefining what "fighting inflation" means.

**Evidence FOR:**
- This is the historical pattern: almost every Fed Chair starts with one posture and pivots
  - Burns: hawkish → dovish (1970s stagflation)
  - Volcker: hawkish → maintained (exception that proves the rule)
  - Greenspan: ambiguous → pragmatically dovish (Great Moderation)
  - Bernanke: hawkish → emergency easing (GFC)
  - Yellen: dovish → gradually tighter
  - Powell: dovish (COVID) → aggressive tightening
- The balance sheet panel provides institutional cover for a gradual pivot
- "Regime change" language is deliberately ambiguous
- Coming out hawkish builds political capital to spend later on easing
- The "negotiating strategy" interpretation: Warsh is establishing his independence NOW so he has credibility to ease LATER without looking like a Trump puppet

**Evidence AGAINST:**
- If Warsh genuinely believes in 2%, the pivot may not come
- The doubled-down rhetoric creates expectations that are hard to reverse
- Political pressure from Trump may not be sustainable (midterms, attention span)

**Curve implication**: 2s10s stays flat at +30-50 for 3-6 months, then steepens to +60-100 as Warsh pivots. Value rotation begins mid-to-late 2027.

**Confirming signals:**
- Balance sheet panel recommends gradual QT with "flexible" framework
- Warsh's language gradually softens over 3-6 months (watch for shifts in testimony/minutes)
- Reserves management purchases start small but grow
- Warsh begins emphasizing "growth" alongside "inflation" in public statements
- Rate hold language shifts from "higher for longer" to "patient"

**Disconfirming signals:**
- Warsh maintains pure hawkish stance for 12+ months
- Balance sheet shrinks aggressively toward $5T
- No expansion of reserves management purchases
- Warsh explicitly rules out any form of QE or easing

## 4. The "Negotiating Strategy" Question

**Question raised**: Is Warsh's aggressive hawkish posture a negotiating tactic — establishing credibility and independence early to give himself more room to maneuver later?

**Analysis**: This is the most sophisticated interpretation. Here's why it works:

1. **The credibility problem**: Warsh was appointed by Trump, who demands rate cuts. If Warsh starts dovish, he looks like a Trump puppet. Congress and the markets won't trust him. By starting hawkish, he establishes independence.

2. **The political capital strategy**: Every hawkish statement now is "money in the bank" for later. When Warsh eventually eases (and he will, because the debt and growth dynamics demand it), he can say "I fought inflation first — now I'm calibrating." The hawkish start buys dovish credibility.

3. **The Powell contrast**: Powell was seen as reactive and politically pressured. Warsh is differentiating himself by being PROACTIVELY hawkish. This makes him harder to attack when he eventually pivots.

4. **The balance sheet panel timing**: The panel gives Warsh 3-6 months of "we're studying the issue" cover. During this time, he can maintain hawkish rhetoric while the staff work develops the operational framework for shadow easing. When the panel reports, the "regime change" has institutional backing.

**Assessment**: The negotiating strategy interpretation supports **Hypothesis C (45%)** — Warsh is building credibility now to enable a pragmatic pivot later. This is the Greenspan playbook: establish your inflation-fighting bona fides, then ease when the conditions demand it.
